## Human-collaborative AI
KICKER: AI SHOULD ASSIST / HUMANS SHOULD LEAD
LEDE: Kramaniti designs the boundary, not only the model call.

### Automate
Use deterministic automation for repetitive, low-ambiguity steps with clear failure handling.

### Assist
Use AI to summarize, classify, draft, compare, or surface options where a person remains responsible for the decision.

### Keep human-led
Retain human ownership where context, trust, taste, negotiation, ethics, or irreversible consequences matter.

### Required controls
Every system should define inputs, review checkpoints, override rules, exception paths, data boundaries, and a named owner.

<!-- layout: mechanics | theme: light | section: VERBAL IDENTITY -->
## Writing mechanics
KICKER: THE RHYTHM OF THE VOICE
LEDE: Short conclusions and explicit sequences create clarity without flattening depth.

### Lead
Open with the conclusion, decision, or problem. Do not begin with general commentary about AI.

### Structure
Use one idea per paragraph. Prefer three-part sequences, compact lists, and meaningful subheads.

### Detail
Name the workflow, role, handoff, system, or review point instead of using broad transformation language.

### Length
Keep first-impression copy compact. Allow depth in articles, audits, and implementation documents.

### Punctuation
Use sentence case. Avoid excessive exclamation marks, decorative punctuation, and all-caps headlines.

<!-- layout: logo_rules | theme: light | section: VISUAL IDENTITY -->
## Clear space and minimum size
KICKER: RECOMMENDED PRODUCTION STANDARD
LEDE: Give the mark room to hold its shape, especially against detailed or luminous backgrounds.

### Clear space
Keep at least 0.25 times the visible mark width clear on every side. Use more space in premium editorial layouts.

### Digital minimum
Do not render the symbol smaller than 24 px. Use 32 px or more in navigation and 48 px or more in hero or document contexts.

### Print minimum
Do not reproduce the symbol below 8 mm wide. Verify fine edges in a physical proof.

### Background
Use on Obsidian, Graphite, warm white, or a quiet image area with sufficient contrast. Do not place on visually noisy detail.

- 5-10% Burnished Gold and supporting accents

## Typography family
KICKER: ONE GEOMETRIC VOICE / ONE TECHNICAL ACCENT
LEDE: The live Kramaniti system uses Outfit for display and reading, with JetBrains Mono for coordinates and system detail.

### Outfit
Use Regular for body, Medium for compact emphasis, SemiBold for section titles, and Bold or ExtraBold for primary headlines.

### JetBrains Mono
Use for numbers, coordinates, statuses, metadata, file references, and technical annotations.

### Fallback
Use Inter, Poppins, or a clean system sans only when Outfit is unavailable. Keep the hierarchy and spacing intact.

### Avoid
Do not mix decorative serif, script, condensed display, or multiple geometric sans families in one asset.

## Type composition
KICKER: EDITORIAL HIERARCHY
LEDE: A composition should feel calm before it feels clever.

### Headline
Use sentence case, a tight line height, and a balanced maximum width. Avoid all-caps primary headlines.

### Lead
Place one short explanatory line under the title. Keep it under roughly 680 px or 72 characters.

### Body
Use short paragraphs, generous line spacing, and visible separation between ideas.

### Micro-label
Uppercase is allowed only for small coordinates and section labels. Use JetBrains Mono or Outfit SemiBold with tracked spacing.

### Emphasis
Highlight one noun, number, or decision in gold. Do not color every keyword.

## Layout grid
KICKER: STRUCTURE THAT CAN BREATHE
LEDE: Grids provide alignment; negative space provides authority.

### Desktop
Use a 12-column grid within a 1200 px maximum container and 24-32 px gutters.

### Mobile
Use a 4-column grid with 16-20 px outer margins and at least 16 px between modules.

### Editorial
Use a 6- or 12-column landscape grid. Let headlines span fewer columns than diagrams or tables.

### Alignment
Choose one dominant axis per composition. Avoid centering every element when the content needs a reading path.

### Negative space
Reserve at least 30% of high-impact imagery and section openings as quiet space.

## Spacing, surfaces, and radius
KICKER: SOFT DEPTH / CONTROLLED GEOMETRY
LEDE: The system should feel dimensional without becoming glossy or ornamental.

### Spacing
Use an 8 px base rhythm. Preferred intervals: 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 120.

### Radius
Use 8 px for buttons and controls, 18-28 px for expressive panels, and full pills only for compact actions or statuses.

### Surfaces
Use Graphite or Charcoal with a subtle one-pixel border. Light mode relies on soft shadow and tonal separation.

### Glass
Glass effects should be quiet, readable, and limited to one enclosure layer. Avoid stacked translucency and heavy blur.

### Shadow
Use broad, low-opacity elevation. Do not use hard black drop shadows or colored neon glows.

## Flow-line system
KICKER: THE SIGNATURE MOTION LANGUAGE
LEDE: Thin rails and moving signals make sequence visible without turning the page into a circuit-board cliche.

### Geometry
Use one-pixel horizontal or vertical lines, occasional right-angle paths, and clear intersections.

### Signal
Place a small warm node or traveling highlight on a line to show progress, focus, or handoff.

### Opacity
Keep static rails between 8% and 34% opacity. The active signal may reach full gold or warm white briefly.

### Motion
Use varied durations and negative delays so lines feel alive on entry. Horizontal motion travels left to right; vertical motion travels top to bottom.

### Static translation
In print, freeze the signal near a meaningful junction and use the line as a divider, path, or coordinate frame.

## Nodes, orbits, and grids
KICKER: SUPPORTING MOTIFS
LEDE: Each motif carries a different kind of relationship.

### Nodes
Represent decisions, handoffs, inputs, outputs, or moments of human review.

### Orbits
Represent connected layers such as strategy, systems, and communication around a shared foundation.

### Grids
Represent order, measurement, and a field of possible routes. Keep them faint and incomplete.

### Rails
Represent sequence and continuity. Use them for processes, timelines, and staged explanations.

### Rule
Do not combine every motif on one screen. Choose the smallest visual language that makes the relationship clear.

## Atmospheric words
KICKER: MEANING AT THE EDGE OF ATTENTION
LEDE: Oversized words can anchor a section without competing with the headline.

### Approved direction
Use short, semantically relevant words such as Clarity, Focus, Method, Layers, Offer, Build, Flow, Run, Trust, or Start.

### Treatment
Set in Outfit at very large scale, low opacity, or transparent fill with a quiet gold outline.

### Placement
Crop partially at a page or viewport edge and keep the readable headline in the foreground.

### Limit
Use one or two atmosphere words per section. Do not create decorative word clouds or repeat the page title at full opacity.

## Iconography
KICKER: SIMPLE LINES / CONSISTENT GEOMETRY
LEDE: Icons explain actions and states. They should not carry the identity by themselves.

### Style
Use open line icons with 1.5 px strokes, rounded caps, and consistent optical weight.

### Color
Use Silver Mist by default. Use Burnished Gold for active, selected, or focal states.

### Sources
Prefer Lucide or Phosphor when a standard interface symbol is needed.

### Custom symbols
Build custom diagrams from rails, nodes, brackets, and measured geometry rather than illustrative 3D icons.

### Avoid
No emoji as interface icons, no filled cartoon sets, no skeuomorphic objects, and no mixed icon families.

## Photography direction
KICKER: HUMAN DEPTH / CONTROLLED LIGHT
LEDE: Photography should feel observed, spatial, and real - never like a generic corporate stock library.

### Light
Use low-key, directional light with warm amber highlights against cool or neutral shadows.

### Space
Create foreground, midground, and background separation. Use leading lines and at least 30% negative space.

### Human presence
Show real gestures, work, attention, and imperfections. Avoid staged handshakes or groups smiling at a laptop.

### Lens language
Use 35 mm for environments and 85 mm for portraits. Keep depth of field intentional.

### Finish
Use restrained film grain and natural texture. Remove synthetic AI markers, impossible reflections, and overly smooth surfaces.

## Data and diagrams
KICKER: EVIDENCE BEFORE DECORATION
LEDE: Visualisation should make a relationship easier to understand than prose alone.

### Charts
Use Ice White or Ink for the baseline series and Burnished Gold for the single important comparison.

### Labels
Place labels near the data. Avoid detached legends when direct annotation is possible.

### Axes
Use thin Smoke or muted rules. Remove unnecessary ticks, boxes, and background decoration.

### Diagrams
Use a clear start, flow direction, decision gate, and outcome. Show human review explicitly.

### Claims
Every number needs a source, date, unit, and scope. Do not create decorative metrics.

## Motion and accessibility
KICKER: GUIDE ATTENTION / PRESERVE CONTROL
LEDE: Motion should reveal sequence and depth while keeping the static experience complete.

### Motion vocabulary
Soft reveal, slow parallax, rail sweep, node pulse, orbital drift, and subtle hover lift.

### Timing
Use 200-300 ms for direct controls, 400-650 ms for content reveals, and slower loops for ambient motion.

### Easing
Controls should respond quickly. Atmospheric elements may use gentle ease-in-out or linear travel.

### Reduced motion
When reduced motion is requested, remove looping movement and preserve hierarchy, focus, and meaning.

### Accessibility
Maintain keyboard focus, semantic structure, readable contrast, descriptive labels, and non-motion alternatives.

One argument per slide. Use large type, simple diagrams, and a visible decision path.

## Asset governance
KICKER: EXISTENCE IS NOT PERMISSION
LEDE: Every reusable asset needs a source, owner, status, and approved context.

### Public-ready
Approved for repeated use in the stated contexts.

### Internal-only
Available for private planning, drafts, and internal references.

### Needs review
Source, rights, quality, or channel context still needs confirmation.

### Retired
Do not use without explicit reapproval.

### Required record
Asset name, path, type, creator or source, permission status, approved channels, proof sensitivity, and review date where relevant.

## Claim and proof governance
KICKER: SOURCE / SCOPE / PERMISSION / WORDING
LEDE: A claim is publishable only when its evidence and context support the exact wording.

### Review
Identify whether the material contains a client name, logo, metric, testimonial, selected work, founder proof, or external research.

### Verify
Check the primary source, date, unit, scope, and permission status.

### Calibrate
Use exact wording for the available evidence. Soften to category-level language when proof is limited.

### Approve
Founder approval is required for public proof, pricing, promises, testimonials, client references, and sensitive founder claims.

### Record
Update the proof register so the decision survives beyond the current conversation.

## Quality gate
KICKER: BEFORE AN ASSET LEAVES DRAFT
LEDE: The final check is strategic, verbal, visual, technical, and evidentiary.

### Strategy
Does the work follow strategy before tools, systems before scale, and content after clarity?

### Voice
Is it specific, business-first, useful, and free of generic AI-agency language?

### Visual
Are the mark, palette, typography, spacing, contrast, and hierarchy correct?

### Proof
Are claims sourced, permission-aware, and calibrated to the evidence?

### Technical
Does the artifact render correctly across its intended sizes, formats, devices, or pages?

### Continuity
Can another person find the source, understand the decision, and produce the next version?

## Production and file standards
KICKER: KEEP THE SYSTEM FINDABLE
LEDE: Good production hygiene makes the brand easier to scale without visual or factual drift.

### Naming
Use descriptive snake_case names and stable version labels.

### Source and export
Keep an editable source when possible. Store approved exports under the brand asset library.

### Logo
Use the registered artwork. Do not rasterize more than necessary or export from screenshots.

### Color
Use sRGB for digital output. Verify CMYK conversions and physical proofs before commercial print.

### Images
Keep adequate resolution, intentional crops, and provenance notes. Avoid repeated lossy compression.

### Review
Render and inspect the real output. A successful export is not proof of a polished artifact.
