# Find where work waits by asking the people who do it

Version 1.1 · Kramaniti Kosh

## Intended outcome

A short interview record that shows where work waits, repeats or depends on one person, so you can choose what to fix before you buy or build anything.

## Before you begin

- Permission from the owner to talk with the people who do the work
- One recurring process that already feels slow or fragile
- A quiet place and enough time for a short, honest conversation

## How to use

1. Choose one process and book two short conversations: the founder or owner, and one frontline person who does the work day to day.
2. Ask where work waits, what gets repeated and who is needed for each stuck point. Write what you hear; leave blanks visible rather than guessing.
3. Agree one bottleneck to address first, name an owner and what better would look like. Money, legal exposure or a customer promise stays with a named human the same day.

## Who to ask

Speak with both of these people when you can.

- The founder or owner who feels the delay
- One frontline person who actually does the work (reception, chair-side, warehouse, ops or similar)

If only one conversation is possible, prefer the frontline person and note that the founder view is still missing.

## Questions to ask

Ask in plain language. Stay with what happened recently, not the ideal process.

- Where does work sit waiting before the next person can start?
- What do you have to do twice, or redo, in a normal week?
- Which steps only move when one named person is available?
- What do you check by memory, chat or a scrap of paper that is not written down?
- If one small change could remove a wait this month, what would it be?

## Capture table

Record one row per step or task you hear. Leave cells blank when the answer is unclear.

- Step or task
- What happens
- Where it waits
- Who is needed
- How often (only if they said so; otherwise leave blank)

## Stop rules

- Do not invent tools, products or vendors from the interview notes.
- Do not treat interview notes as proof of cost, frequency or revenue impact.
- Money, legal wording or a promise to a customer stays with a named human owner.
- If the founder and the frontline person disagree, record both views; do not force a single story.

## Demonstration

This is an illustrative scenario, not a client case study or a measured result. Do not reuse its details as facts about your work.

A sample four-person family-run dental clinic in Bengaluru runs three chairs. The owners feel that patient flow and follow-up are slow, but nobody has yet asked the people at reception and chair-side where work actually sticks.

### Sample inputs

Demonstration inputs only: two short interviews about the path from enquiry to booked appointment and day-of check-in. Roles asked: clinic owner and reception lead. No timing studies, cost figures or patient outcomes are available.

## Quality check

Both who-to-ask roles are covered or the gap is noted; the capture table has visible blanks where answers were unclear; one bottleneck has interview evidence, a named owner and a review date; no tools, costs or frequencies were invented.

## Limits and human review

The interview shows where people say work waits; it does not prove cost, frequency or the right tool. Notes are not a substitute for clinical, legal or financial judgment. Keep consequential actions with the named human owner.
