# Follow up on unpaid invoices without hurting the relationship

Version 1.1 · Kramaniti Kosh

> How to use this file: fill in the Working template section below. It opens in any text editor, or you can paste the whole file into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and ask it to help you fill it in for your business.

## Intended outcome

A short brief that lets an assistant draft polite, dated payment reminders from your list of unpaid invoices, while a named person approves every reminder before it goes out and handles any dispute, so money gets chased on time without straining a good customer relationship.

## Before you begin

- Your current list of unpaid invoices (a sheet or an export from your accounting software) with invoice number, date, amount, due date and customer contact
- The payment terms you agreed with each customer, as written on the order, contract or invoice
- One person who will approve reminders and speak to customers when something is disputed

## How to use

1. Fill in the business details, the reminder steps and the tone rules below. Keep the steps to what you would be comfortable saying to the customer's face.
2. Give the assistant the brief and the invoice list. Ask it to draft only the reminders that are due today under your steps, each one quoting the invoice number, date, amount and due date from the list.
3. The named person reads each draft against the invoice list, edits or holds it, and sends it themselves. Anything the customer disputes moves to the dispute route and stops getting reminders.

## What the assistant may and may not do

The assistant may:

- Draft reminders that follow the steps above, using only details from the invoice list
- Point out invoices with missing details, wrong contacts or amounts that do not match
- List which invoices are due for which step today

The assistant may not:

- Send anything on its own
- Change an amount, a due date or the payment terms
- Mention interest, penalties, legal notices or any law unless the owner has written that exact wording
- Offer discounts, payment plans or extensions
- Reply to a customer who disputes the invoice

## Stop rules

- No reminder goes out without the named person reading it against the invoice list first.
- If an invoice is disputed, or the customer has already promised a payment date, stop drafting reminders for it until the owner says otherwise.
- Do not invent amounts, dates, interest figures or past conversations. If a detail is missing from the list, leave it blank and flag it.
- Do not mention legal remedies, interest under any law or a complaint to any authority unless the owner has checked the wording with an accountant or lawyer.
- If a reminder would feel wrong to send to this customer in person, do not send it.

## Quality check

Every reminder quotes the invoice number, date, amount and due date exactly as in the list; each draft matches a written step; disputed invoices and excluded customers got no drafts; no interest, penalty or legal wording appears without owner approval; the named person read and sent each reminder; missing details were flagged, not guessed.

## Limits and human review

The template helps you chase payments politely and on time; it does not give legal or accounting advice, check what a customer actually owes, or decide whether any payment law applies to you. Ask your accountant or lawyer before mentioning interest or legal remedies. A filled template is not approval to send reminders, change terms or contact a customer. Keep consequential actions with the named human owner.
