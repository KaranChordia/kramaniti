# Shortlist job applicants fairly with AI help

Version 1.1 · Kramaniti Kosh

> How to use this file: fill in the Working template section below. It opens in any text editor, or you can paste the whole file into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and ask it to help you fill it in for your business.

## Intended outcome

A one-page set of hiring rules written before any AI tool reads a single application: what the role really needs, what an applicant must have, what must never count against anyone, and who decides. AI can then sort applications into piles against those rules, while a named person reads every pile, decides who to call, reject or hire, and can explain each one.

## Before you begin

- A plain description of the job: what the person will do on a normal day, where, and with whom
- The things someone truly needs on day one, and the things they can learn on the job
- The applications you already have, or the place they arrive (a job portal, email, WhatsApp, a referral list)
- One person who will own the hiring decision and read the AI's sorting before anyone is contacted

## How to use

1. Fill in the role and the rules below before you open any application. Rules written after reading the applicants tend to bend towards whoever you liked first.
2. Give the AI tool only the rules and the application text. Remove photos, and replace names with a reference number before the AI reads the text. Do not ask it to judge things that are not in your must-haves. Ask it to sort each application into "meets the must-haves", "unclear" or "does not meet", and to quote the line from the application that supports each sort.
3. The decision owner reads every pile, including "does not meet", before anyone is called or rejected. Any reject is the owner's decision, not the tool's. Record each decision and the reason in the log.

## Never counts against an applicant

Things the AI tool and the people reviewing must ignore. Check this list with a lawyer or an HR adviser before you use it.

- Gender, age, religion, caste, marital status, disability, or where someone is from
- Name, photo or how a name sounds
- Gaps in work history, unless the role has a stated reason to ask about them
- English fluency, unless the job itself needs it and it is listed in the must-haves

## What the AI tool may do

- Sort applications against the must-haves into "meets", "unclear" or "does not meet"
- Quote the line in each application that supports its sort
- List questions to ask in a first call

## What the AI tool must not do

- Reject, rank by score, or contact any applicant
- Guess at anything the application does not say
- Use anything on the "never counts" list, even as a tiebreaker

## Stop rules

- Do not use the AI sort until the must-haves and the never-counts list are written down and the decision owner has signed them.
- Do not send any rejection, call or offer from the tool. A person sends every message.
- If the owner keeps disagreeing with the AI sort, stop using the tool and rewrite the must-haves.
- Do not paste ID numbers, salary slips or other private documents into an AI tool.
- If an applicant asks how they were assessed, the decision owner answers, using the log.
- Decide how long you keep applications and the log, and delete them after that. This is the owner's decision. Applicants should be told that an AI tool helps sort applications; if you are unsure what you must tell them, ask a lawyer or HR adviser.

## Demonstration

This is an illustrative scenario, not a client case study or a measured result. Do not reuse its details as facts about your work.

A sample rooftop solar installer in Bengaluru is hiring field sales staff to visit homes and apartment associations, explain rooftop systems and book site surveys. Applications come in from a job portal and through WhatsApp referrals. The owner wants AI to help sort them, but worries that the tool will quietly drop good people for the wrong reasons.

### Sample inputs

Demonstration inputs only. No applicant names, counts or results are used. Role notes from the owner: visits homes across the city on a two-wheeler, speaks Kannada and either English or Hindi with customers, can explain an electricity bill, logs visits in a phone app. Two sample applications: one from a person with retail sales experience at an electronics store and a driving licence, whose application does not mention languages; one from a recent graduate with no sales experience who has done a summer job doing door-to-door surveys and lists Kannada and Hindi.

### Example output

#### Must-haves
[Fact within this demonstration] Valid two-wheeler licence. Speaks Kannada and either English or Hindi. Willing to travel across the city daily. Any experience of speaking to customers face to face.

#### Never counts against an applicant
[Fact within this demonstration] Degree or college name, age, gaps in work history, and where someone is from.

#### AI sort
[Fact within this demonstration] The retail applicant is sorted "unclear", quoting the retail experience and licence lines and noting that languages are not mentioned. The survey applicant is also sorted "unclear", quoting the door-to-door survey line and the languages line and noting that a licence is not mentioned.
[Inference] Neither application gives enough to reject. Both missing details can be checked in a short call, which is cheaper than losing a good applicant.

#### Owner's decision
[Recommendation] Call both, ask the same questions about languages, licence and travel, and record the reason for each decision in the log. This recommendation is not the business's decision.

## Quality check

The must-haves were written before any application was read; every must-have can be seen in an application or checked in a call; the never-counts list was checked with a lawyer or HR adviser; every AI sort quotes the application; the decision owner read every pile, including "does not meet"; every call, reject and hire has a reason in the log and a named person who made it; no message went to an applicant from the tool.

## Limits and human review

The template helps you set fair rules before AI sorts applications and keeps every hiring decision with a person; it does not give legal or HR advice, prove that a tool is free of bias, or tell you which hiring laws apply to your business. Check your never-counts list and how you store applicant data with a lawyer or HR adviser. A sorted pile is not a decision to call, reject or hire anyone. Keep consequential actions with the named human owner.
